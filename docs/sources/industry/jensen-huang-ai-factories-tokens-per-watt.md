## Main claim

TODO: State the central argument once exact sources are confirmed.

**Reported characterization (unverified):** Jensen Huang has described the direction of AI infrastructure as a shift from discrete model deployments to continuous "AI factories" — purpose-built infrastructure designed to produce tokens at scale, continuously, and efficiently. In this framing, the primary output of AI infrastructure is tokens, and the primary efficiency metric is tokens per watt (and tokens per dollar).

---

## Relevance to NiceM

The AI factories / tokens-per-watt framing is significant for NiceM because it places token throughput and energy efficiency at the center of AI infrastructure economics — not model quality scores alone.

If the industry measures AI infrastructure efficiency in tokens-per-watt, then:
- Token-tax (which increases tokens required for equivalent semantic content) directly degrades tokens-per-watt for underrepresented languages
- Execution-tax (NiceM hypothesis — which increases tokens required for equivalent task completion) would directly degrade tokens-per-watt for agentic workloads

Both concepts can therefore be expressed in the industry's own efficiency vocabulary, without requiring the introduction of new metrics.

This framing also supports the NiceM concept of **successful intent per watt** as the more meaningful long-term metric: not raw token throughput, but how much useful human intent is completed per unit of energy.

---

## What this supports

- The industry is treating token throughput as the primary AI infrastructure output (context for NiceM's relevance)
- Tokens-per-watt is a real and actively used efficiency metric in AI infrastructure circles
- The economic framing of AI as a factory (continuous production at scale) makes token waste an infrastructure-level cost, not just an API pricing footnote
- This creates a receptive audience for NiceM's argument that execution-tax is an infrastructure problem, not just a developer inconvenience

## Connection to tokens-per-watt

Tokens-per-watt is the direct industry expression of the AI factory efficiency question. Jensen Huang's use of this metric establishes it as a meaningful vocabulary for AI infrastructure operators — not just a technical curiosity.

For NiceM, the relevance is:
- Token-tax increases tokens per semantic unit → reduces tokens-per-watt efficiency for affected languages
- Execution-tax (hypothesis) increases tokens per completed intent → reduces tokens-per-watt efficiency for agentic workloads
- The NiceM long-term metric (successful intent per watt) is the natural extension of tokens-per-watt once task completion quality is added
