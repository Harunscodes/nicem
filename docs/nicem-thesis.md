# NiceM — Thesis Statement

## One-sentence version

> AI systems impose a hidden cost structure on users and infrastructure operators through two distinct mechanisms — token-tax and execution-tax — that are invisible in standard API pricing but measurable, addressable, and commercially significant.

---

## The two-layer cost problem

### Layer 1 — Token-tax (supported by research)

Token-tax is the cost premium imposed when tokenization systems produce more tokens for equivalent semantic content in certain languages, scripts, or input types compared to others.

The mechanism is technical: subword tokenization vocabularies are built predominantly from high-resource languages. As a result, the same meaning expressed in a lower-resource language consumes more tokens, and therefore costs more at the API level.

This is not a pricing decision — it is an emergent structural inequality baked into the tokenization layer. The academic literature (Petrov et al., Ahia et al., Lundin) documents and names this effect.

**Status:** Supported by existing research. Token-tax is an established concept in the NLP fairness literature.

---

### Layer 2 — Execution-tax (NiceM hypothesis)

Execution-tax is a proposed concept: the additional token and compute overhead generated by the structure of agentic, multi-step AI workloads — beyond what the core task semantically requires.

Sources of execution-tax include:
- System prompt repetition across chained calls
- Tool-call scaffolding and response formatting overhead
- Re-tokenization of context across agent steps
- Orchestration tokens that carry no semantic payload

Execution-tax is distinct from token-tax. Token-tax arises at the tokenization layer from language/script disparity. Execution-tax arises at the execution layer from workload architecture.

**Status:** NiceM hypothesis. Not yet proven by external research. Requires measurement and validation. See `docs/open-questions.md`.

---

## Why both matter together

Token-tax and execution-tax compound. A user running an agentic workflow in an underrepresented language pays the token-tax on every token produced, and pays the execution-tax on every overhead token generated by the orchestration structure. The combined cost can be substantially higher than a naive API pricing estimate would suggest.

---

## The industry context

Jensen Huang's framing of AI infrastructure as "AI factories" optimized for tokens-per-watt establishes that the industry already measures AI efficiency in token throughput terms. NiceM applies this metric lens to the cost-fairness and architectural-efficiency problem.

NVIDIA's work on agentic infrastructure shows that multi-step, orchestrated workloads are the direction of the industry — which means execution-tax is not a niche concern but a structural feature of how AI will be deployed at scale.

---

## What NiceM proposes

NiceM is not a complaint about pricing. It is a research and product foundation aimed at:

1. **Measuring** token-tax and execution-tax in real workloads
2. **Attributing** overhead to its structural sources
3. **Reducing** unnecessary execution-tax through workload design
4. **Making visible** the combined cost structure that is currently opaque to users and operators

---

## What NiceM does not claim

- That tokenization disparity is intentional or malicious
- That token-tax and execution-tax are the same phenomenon
- That execution-tax has been measured or proven (it is a hypothesis)
- That any specific company or product is the cause of these effects
